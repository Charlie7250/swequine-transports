# Benchmark Review

## Purpose

This review is not a feature-shopping exercise.

It exists to answer:

- what kind of product behaviour users already expect
- where the current app is still weaker than the available alternatives
- what should shape the near-term portal
- what should shape the longer-term marketplace

## Benchmark set

The benchmark set for this planning layer is:

- HayNet, as the current horse-transport directory and quote-request baseline
- Shiply, as the marketplace and spare-capacity quote pattern
- field-service quoting and routing products, as workflow and routing references for operator productivity

## Summary table

| Benchmark | Customer | Workflow it makes fast | What it currently does better than this app | Planning response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HayNet | Horse owners looking for transport, transporters paying for visibility | Find transporters, send quote requests, advertise transporter listings | Better at discovery, better at quote-request aggregation, more obviously connected to inbound demand | Copy the clarity of enquiry capture and transporter profile value, reject directory-only product limits |
| Shiply | Customers who need transport, transport providers who can quote jobs | List a job, receive multiple transport quotes, benefit from existing route overlap | Better at marketplace demand capture, better at spare-capacity framing, better at “providers come to the job” positioning | Adapt the marketplace pattern later, reject price-race behaviour as the core near-term portal value |
| Field-service quoting and routing software | Service businesses managing daily quoting and routes | Turn incoming job details into a fast quote with route-aware operations | Better at workflow speed, route awareness, and operator-first tooling | Copy operator-speed patterns and route-aware workflow structure for the MVP rescue release |

## HayNet

## Observed positioning

HayNet presents itself as a horse-transport network where users can:

- find horse transporters
- request transport quotes
- advertise as a transporter

It is useful because it solves discovery and enquiry distribution in a niche market that already knows horse transport is a distinct service category.

## Who the customer is

Two-sided, but with obvious transporter-side commercial value:

- horse owners and people moving horses
- transporters paying to be visible and reachable

## What workflow it makes fast

It makes these things relatively simple:

- discover transport providers
- contact multiple providers
- browse transporter listings
- treat quote requests as a lead source

## What it does better than the current app

Compared with the current portal, HayNet is better at:

- being close to real demand
- aggregating enquiries
- presenting transporters as searchable businesses
- making inbound quote requests feel like a normal user flow

## What to copy

- clear inbound enquiry capture
- obvious transporter profile value
- strong “find transport” entry point
- a product model where transporters see value in being present even before full workflow adoption

## What to adapt

- quote-request flow should feed directly into an operator-quality quoting workflow
- listing and enquiry value should later connect to the premium portal tier
- directory behaviour should eventually support quality filters, route coverage, and operator availability

## What to reject

- directory-only value as the end state
- weak operational workflow after enquiry capture
- separation between lead source and quoting system

## Product implication

HayNet is the baseline to beat for:

- transporter discovery
- enquiry capture
- perceived value to transporters paying for visibility

But it is not the benchmark for operator workflow quality.

## Shiply

## Observed positioning

Shiply positions itself as a transport marketplace where customers list a job and transport providers quote for it. Its public value proposition leans heavily on:

- receiving multiple quotes
- saving time versus phoning providers one by one
- benefiting from providers already travelling similar routes

## Who the customer is

Again two-sided:

- customers needing transport
- transport providers quoting on jobs

## What workflow it makes fast

- describe a job once
- receive multiple quotes
- compare providers and prices

## What it does better than the current app

Compared with the current portal, Shiply is better at:

- marketplace-style demand capture
- framing route overlap and existing journeys as a commercial benefit
- turning a request into a competitive quote event

## What to copy

- the concept that route overlap can create stronger lead and quoting value
- customer expectation that multiple providers can respond to one request
- the idea that the platform can broker supply and demand instead of just recording internal quotes

## What to adapt

- route overlap should become a premium transporter-side operational advantage later, not just a lowest-price mechanism
- marketplace flows should preserve trust, licensing, and horse-welfare expectations in this niche
- spare-capacity logic should connect to route intent and future shared-load opportunities

## What to reject

- commoditised price-race behaviour as the main strategic position
- generic delivery-marketplace assumptions that ignore equine welfare, specialist licensing, and trust
- near-term product drift away from the operator portal

## Product implication

Shiply is the right mental model for:

- later marketplace mechanics
- later route-aware matching
- later lead distribution logic

It is not the right near-term model for the MVP rescue release, which must prioritise operator workflow over open-market competition.

## Field-service quoting and routing products

This category includes route-aware quoting, field-service CRM, and service-operations products that make it easy for staff to turn a request into a priced, operational record quickly.

## Why this category matters

This is the strongest workflow benchmark category for the near-term portal because these products are built around:

- incoming work
- route context
- operator speed
- transition from quote to job

Those are exactly the operational qualities this portal still lacks in practice.

## Who the customer is

- service businesses
- dispatchers
- owners
- operational staff

## What workflow it makes fast

- capture the incoming job
- price it
- place it into the operational system
- manage route and follow-up context

## What it does better than the current app

Compared with the current portal, good field-service tools are better at:

- treating route context as a core part of the product
- making quoting fast enough for real operational use
- designing screens around the user’s job instead of around internal domain structures
- making operational transitions feel like part of one system

## What to copy

- operator-first quote workflow
- fast form progression
- route-aware data as a first-class concept
- clear transitions from quote to operational follow-up
- metrics around quoting speed and exception rate

## What to adapt

- horse-transport pricing remains leg-based and domain-specific
- welfare, trust, and compliance details matter more here than in generic field service
- shared-load concepts must fit equine transport reality, not generic van routing

## What to reject

- generic service templates that flatten the horse-transport domain
- enterprise complexity that slows down a small operator team
- route optimisation work that jumps ahead of quoting automation

## Product implication

This category should shape the near-term experience more than HayNet or Shiply should.

For the MVP rescue release, the product should behave more like a route-aware field-service quoting tool than a broad marketplace.

## Copy, adapt, reject summary

## Copy now

- clear enquiry capture from HayNet
- operator-speed workflow from field-service quoting tools
- route-aware product posture from field-service tools

## Adapt later

- marketplace lead flow from Shiply
- spare-capacity and route-overlap value from Shiply
- transporter profile and listing value from HayNet

## Reject

- permanent manual route-mile workflow
- directory-only product strategy
- race-to-the-bottom marketplace positioning
- broad feature sprawl before the quoting flow is fast and reliable

## Strategic conclusion

The immediate product should not try to feel like a marketplace yet.

It should first become:

- the fastest trustworthy quoting workflow for one horse transport business

Then it can become:

- the premium workflow layer that gives a later transporter marketplace real retention power.
